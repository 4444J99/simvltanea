## One composition, two spatial arrangements

The distinction is:

**Content:** which independent video loops are playing, and how they evolve over time.  
**Presentation:** how those same loops are arranged within the vertical or horizontal canvas.

Adding a panel adds another loop—another moving element with its own duration, playback position, and temporal relationship to the others. Changing orientation rearranges the composition; it should not change its constituent loops.

The required initial design matrix is therefore:

| Composition | Vertical / portrait | Horizontal / landscape |
|---|---|---|
| **3 video loops** | Designed three-panel arrangement | Designed three-panel arrangement |
| **4 video loops** | Designed four-panel arrangement | Designed four-panel arrangement |
| **5 video loops** | Designed five-panel arrangement | Designed five-panel arrangement |
| **6 video loops** | Designed six-panel arrangement | Designed six-panel arrangement |
| **Each additional count** | Its own vertical arrangement | Its own horizontal arrangement |

**Six is not the ceiling.** It is the end of the first test set. The architecture must accommodate further counts without creating another application for each one.

## “Designed” means more than resized

The horizontal composition should not simply be squeezed into a phone screen. Each orientation needs intentional decisions about **panel proportions, placement, spacing, visual hierarchy, cropping, and the relationships between neighboring images**.

Nor does the requirement force equal-sized grids. Equal panels, asymmetrical arrangements, a dominant panel with smaller companions, and other configurations remain available design choices—not defaults to impose before seeing the work.

This applies to **each arrangement variant**, too. A five-loop equal-weight arrangement and a five-loop asymmetrical arrangement would each need their own portrait/landscape pair.

## The loops must survive the change of orientation

Rotating a device or resizing the window should preserve the same sources, loop identities, playback progression, and interaction state. It must not restart the work, reshuffle the videos, or drop panels to make the layout fit.

Likewise, adding the sixth loop should not restart the preceding five.

“Mobile” and “desktop” describe the viewing contexts, but the presentation should respond to the actual available shape: a phone can be horizontal, and a desktop browser window can be tall and narrow.
